# WEIRWATER - Arc Skeletons (GM)

Skeletons, not scripts. Three mainlines run on their own clocks and cross through the same small country; any one carried to an ending is a campaign "won," and the endings recolor each other. The contracts are the economy and the teacher: the bestiary's cases train the player's craft until the arcs demand everything the craft has learned. The first act's hard clock is civic, not mythic: the Margravine's debt falls due at Candlenight, ~40 days in.

## Arc 1: THE WAKE-CHILD (the mythic spine)

- **Truth (HIDDEN)**: when the Collegium broke the Hollow Muster, the working's residue did not disperse - it pooled, and it took root in the only living thing born on the Field of Rushes that winter: a child, birthed on the grave-field by a refugee who died of the doing. The child is now six, walking west along the refugee roads with whatever family fragment currently has it, and it does not know what it is. The seam of the war travels WITH it: wrought quicken in its wake, and worse, they COPY - the unnamed (see bestiary) are the Muster trying to re-muster, using the child as its living parchment.
- **Hook**: the pattern - hollow-soldier and unnamed cases thickening along a line on the map that Garrick or the player draws first. Then the failed case: terms done right that do not hold. Then the rumor with a shape: "a lucky child on the east road; the wolves would not touch the column it walked in."
- **Escalation**: the Collegium notices the same pattern (Weiss's map is two weeks behind the player's); the Wake declares the child blessed and gathers a foot-pilgrimage around it; Ordane, who has been LOOKING for where his working pooled since the peace, starts moving toward it with instruments in hand (arcs cross: the Author). Every faction wants the child; almost none of them wants a CHILD.
- **The guild question at the core**: the child is a knot - the biggest ever tied, with the war itself for a cause. Read it deep (the Toll price is terrible) and the terms are legible and enormous: THE WAR ITSELF, BURIED RIGHT. Not a metaphor: the Rushes given full rites - every levy named from both armies' rolls, the Wake officiating at scale, Carthagne and Ostravend consenting on the same field. A diplomatic, logistical, spiritual labor of a final act - the pack's great ending, and the March's actual healing.
- **Endings**: THE GREAT WAKE (terms kept at scale: the seam closes over years, the child grows up ordinary; the ending the whole doctrine points at); THE SHEPHERD (no burial - the player raises or guards the child so the seam never falls into worse hands; the campaign becomes stewardship, and the March stays wounded but held); THE COLLEGIUM'S JAR (hand the child over: the seam is contained, studied, and owned by an empire - safe, cold, and the March's thin places start being licensed); THE KNIFE (the terms refused, the child ended: the seam disperses back into the whole March - the pre-war ratio returns over years, and the player carries forever what the Reading showed them at the last; butcher-grain, world-scale; STAGING LAW: hold this at the latch-child's register - the Reading's testimony and the aftermath, never physical detail; the scene is a wound, not a spectacle); ORDANE'S PARCHMENT (the Author reaches the child first: see Arc 3's bad ending).

## Arc 2: THE LAST WEIR (home, debt, and the Cup)

- **Truth (HIDDEN)**: Blacklade owes the Margravate 300 silver marks - war-tax arrears Garrick signed to keep the house alive, due Candlenight, and he has been paying interest by quietly selling the guild library to Sjunna as "rendering stock" (she has sold nothing on; her ledger marks every volume "held"). Worse: the last two Cup candidates before Sile died wrong - not the usual three-in-five weeping death but convulsive, fast. The Cup was poisoned. Whitstan's old dissolution playbook: Carthagne retired that house a generation ago by first making its ordeal unsurvivable, and Lector Weiss's predecessor ran it. Weiss has the file. The Margravine has NOT ordered it this time - someone local is freelancing the old method (her clerk-of-licenses, Odo Brant, buying his promotion with the guild's extinction).
- **Hook**: the letter on Garrick's desk in the opening scene (the debt, the Candlenight date, the licensing offer that would make Blacklade a Collegium sub-office). Sile is due her first solo contract; Pip wants the Cup someday; the roof leaks over the library that is quietly not there.
- **Escalation**: earning toward 300 marks contract by contract (the economy arc - every bestiary case matters twice); discovery of the library sales (Garrick's shame, Sjunna's strange mercy); the poisoned-Cup thread when the player or Sile investigates the candidate deaths as a CASE, with the study system, exactly like a monster - the pack's thesis applied to people: find the cause, find the terms.
- **Endings**: THE WEIR HOLDS (debt paid or renegotiated, Brant exposed with proof, the Cup drawn true again - Sile takes journeyman, Pip's question becomes real, and the house has a future exactly as fragile as the March); THE LICENSE (Blacklade signs: solvent, legal, leashed - Weiss is now the player's superior, and the doctrine bends a little every season; playable on, deliberately gray); THE LAST LAMP (the debt defaults: the house dissolves, Garrick retires to the fen's edge, and the player hunts on as the March's last freeholder - the guild becomes one person's Mark); THE OPEN GATE (the Cindermoor choice: the house spent deliberately - its remaining coin, credibility, and roof - on the Wake-Child's Great Wake or the road-people's winter; the guild dies doing doctrine, and the March never forgets it).

## Arc 3: THE AUTHOR (Ordane, Mirre's war)

- **Truth (HIDDEN)**: Velm Ordane did not burn. He walked out of the peace with his notes in his head and six of the Muster's INSTRUMENTS - focus-objects the working was written through (a muster-roll that reads its own names, a drum, a surveyor's chain that measured the Rushes, three lesser) - scattered for safekeeping with people who mostly do not know what they hold. He has spent six years retrieving them, patient, polite, unremarkable. His purpose (HIDDEN until late): not conquest - CORRECTION. He believes the Muster failed because it was rushed, and that done properly it would not raise the dead but SETTLE them, all of them, everywhere, permanently: the Wake's paradise by force of working. He is the pack's mirror-thesis: terms for the whole world, imposed. He was also, twenty years ago, the polite young man who asked the moss-hag good questions - and Mirre Kaltag's teacher, which is the license she burned and the man she has hunted since the Rushes, where her hedge-circuit was conscripted to hold the counter-working's southern anchor and she alone walked away.
- **Hook**: Mirre's questions under her errands ("who bought the old garrison drum at the Drossen auction?"); a contract that is secretly an instrument-retrieval gone wrong (the drum's new owner's house has a hollow soldier standing PERFECT watch at its door - wrong war-year, wrong everything).
- **Escalation**: instruments as case-magnets (each one warps its holder's local wrought); Weiss hunting the same objects with a bounty-book and no bedside manner; Mirre forced to reveal what she knows piece by piece as the player gets ahead of her; Ordane, eventually, in a room with the player, being reasonable - the pack's best villain scene is a negotiation, and he offers terms.
- **Endings**: THE ROLLS CLOSED (Ordane stopped and the instruments unmade at the weir - each unmaking is a terms-scene with the war itself, and Mirre's knot unties or does not, by how the player carried her); THE TRIBUNAL (Ordane delivered alive to Collegium justice: lawful, public, and the Empire now owns everything he knows - Weiss's star rises, and so does the licensing of everything); THE SECOND MUSTER (Ordane reaches the Wake-Child with instruments in hand: the campaign's dark climax - the Great Working attempted, and the player at the southern anchor where Mirre once stood, deciding what breaks and who holds); THE AUTHOR'S TERMS (the heretical ending: the player, Reading in hand, concludes Ordane's correction can be MADE TRUE at a price the March can survive, and helps him - written to be genuinely arguable, and the chronicle it produces will argue it forever).

## Companion seeds (full dossiers in npcs.md)

- **Mirre Kaltag**: Arc 3 made person. Romance-capable, on terms - hers, exact, renegotiated honestly or not at all.
- **Tally Voss**: the roads' memory. Her ballad-ledger is a lore-engine; her debts are a road-event engine; her desertion is a hidden knot with terms of its own.
- **Brother Halm**: the Wake made person; the Great Wake ending needs him, and he does not know it yet.
- **Sile / Garrick / Pip**: the guild's past, present, and question - Arc 2 walking around the house.
- **Odd Sjunna**: the gray market made person, holding the library "held," and the only character who deals with everyone in all three arcs.
- **Burr**: yes, the horse. Kit lives on him (kit homes law), his war-sense is a real instrument the fiction may consult, and he is written into more endings than some people.
