# WEIRWATER - The Deep Lore (GM)

The March's history, cosmology, faith, and folkways. Player-facing pieces are marked COMMON (any local knows it), LEARNED (books, priests, guild teaching), or HIDDEN (GM truth; reaches play only through discovery). Feed lore diegetically: a proverb in a tavern, a ballad verse from Tally, a margin note in the Weirhouse ledgers - never a lecture.

## The world before the war (COMMON in outline, LEARNED in detail)

The Veldmark is old drowned country: black alder fens, rye ridges, and the slow river Duna braided through it all, crossed by weirs the first settlers built so long ago that the stones have names. It has always been THIN in places. The old people call the inhabitants of the thin places the **Quiet Neighbors** and never anything ruder: the moss-hags, the barrow-folk, the lights on the fen. The rules for living beside them are older than the faith and simpler than law: greet what greets you, pay what you promise, bury what you kill, and never cut wood at a thin evening (dusk on the quarter-days, when the year turns on its hinge).

For centuries the March belonged to the Kingdom of **Ostravend**, east across the Duna - lightly, the way a coat owns its hem. The Empire of **Carthagne** rose in the west over two hundred years, ate its neighbors politely, and wanted the river trade.

## The Ten Winters' War (COMMON - everyone over ten lived it)

Year 1: Carthagne crossed the Duna at Gris claiming a customs insult. Years 2 through 5: the March changed hands four times; Cindermoor Weirhouse burned with its gates open, sheltering villagers from both sides. Year 6: the Rye Famine - more died of the winter than the war, and the **rye-mothers** rose in the starved fields for the first time in living memory. Years 7 through 9: stalemate, partisans, plague crosses on every third door, the roads unsafe even for the Salt-Marked. Year 10, the last winter: Ostravend, breaking, let its court magus do what he had promised he could.

**The Hollow Muster** (COMMON as horror-story, LEARNED as fact, HIDDEN in mechanism): Velm Ordane, First Reader to the Ostravend crown, wrote a working that called the dead levies of ten winters back into their ranks. It was not necromancy as the hedge knows it - crude, one grave at a time. It was a MUSTER: one working, the whole March as its parchment, every unquiet body a signature. The dead stood up at the Field of Rushes and won it. Carthagne's Collegium broke the working three days later at a cost it has never published, Ostravend's king signed the peace with a hand that never stopped shaking, and Ordane's name went onto the Proscribed List, presumed burned with his tower.

**What the Muster left** (LEARNED, guild doctrine): a working of that size does not break, it TEARS. The March's old thinness is now a worn seam. The dead do not settle without full rites. Grief takes bodies faster. Oaths bind harder and break worse. The guild's ledgers are unambiguous: before the war, Blacklade retired eight or ten wrought a year; the year after the peace, ninety.

## The Weirwatch (LEARNED - guild teaching, told properly)

Founded two hundred years ago after the Drowning of Fenholm village, when the March learned that pitchforks make more wrought than they end. The founders were weir-keepers - hence the name, and the doctrine: a weir does not stop the river, it READS it, and takes only what comes down.

**The five houses and their fates**: BLACKLADE on the Duna weir (stands; three souls). CINDERMOOR on the western moss (burned year 5, gates open; its lame survivor is Garrick Veld). HARROWGATE in the eastern hills (unheard from since the peace; the road east is not what it was). WHITSTAN in Carthagne proper (dissolved by imperial edict twenty years back, its hunters licensed into the Collegium's service or retired; the precedent the Margravine now cites). FENHOLM on the south fen (drowned with its village in the founders' time; its bell is said to ring under the water on thin evenings, and hunters do not fish there).

**The Weeping Cup** (LEARNED; the liturgy is guild-private): the ordeal that makes a hunter. Grave-brine drawn from beneath a weir at the thin evening, one cup, drunk cold. Three in five candidates die weeping, which is where the name comes from and why the guild never sugars it. Survivors rise brine-eyed: the pale iris, the slow aging, and the READING - the sight of residue, of what was done clinging to what it made. The Cup's brine must be drawn fresh and true; the working in it is old, older than the guild, and nobody living knows its author. That last sentence is doctrine, and it is about to matter (see secrets: the poisoned Cup).

**The Reading and the Toll** (LEARNED): the Reading is not free sight, it is EXCHANGE. Deep-read a nature and something small is paid across, permanently: a color, a taste, a name, the warmth of one hand. Guild teaching orders the payments recorded in the hunter's own ledger-hand, because a hunter who loses track of what he has lost starts losing things he did not choose. At seven payments the Quiet begins - a hush under sound, voices in it on still nights, always polite. At twelve the Salt takes the rest: the hunter walks into the fen or sits down at the weir and is, in the old formula, "with the Neighbors now." Retired hunters past nine payments live at the houses and do not take contracts. There are none such left but Garrick, at nine, still working, which every hunter who knows the number understands to be a slow argument with the fen.

**Doctrine, as taught at Blacklade** (LEARNED; the pack's thesis): *the cause before the blade.* Steel is the tool of last certainty. A hunter who kills what could be settled has done half a job at double blood, and made the next knot tighter: the wrought remember, in aggregate, how they are treated. Terms first where terms exist. And the counter-lesson, taught the same hour, always: *some knots are only rope.* A thing past terms is past them - hesitation in front of a hollow soldier is how Cindermoor's survivors died after the fire. The whole craft is telling the difference, and the Reading is how, and the Reading costs. That is the trade.

## The Wake (COMMON - the March's faith)

The March's religion is a funerary one: the **Wake**, keeping of the Lady of Still Waters, who receives the dead "downriver" if - only if - they are buried right: washed, named aloud, weighted with a river-stone, and waked one full night with the door open. The faith's whole liturgy is burial done properly and promises kept to the dead; its monasteries keep the March's death-registers, which makes Wakehouses the best archive a hunter has. Priests dig; that is the order's dignity. Brother Halm was defrocked not for burying enemy dead - the Wake commands that - but for doing it during an interdict, ahead of a battle, on ground the army had claimed, and refusing to stop. The Wake's proverb pair is the March in two lines: *"The river keeps what we do not,"* and *"Cheap graves are dear."*

## The powers now (COMMON)

**The Margravate of Gris**: Ulla Carel, Margravine, Carthagne's administrator - competent, unsentimental, governing a province the Empire wants profitable and quiet. Her three instruments: taxes (ruinous, punctual), the **Edict of Licenses** (all practitioners of "irregular arts" must license or retire - the law that dissolved Whitstan a generation ago, now aimed at Blacklade via its war-debt, due Candlenight), and her Collegium magus in residence, **Lector Abram Weiss**, whose actual commission is hunting the war's proscribed workings and whose private list has one name at the top. The Margravine is not a villain; she is a government. That is its own kind of weather.

**The Collegium**: Carthagne's licensed order of magi - academic, wealthy, contemptuous of hedge-craft, and carrying an unpublished debt: what it cost them to break the Muster at the Rushes. Standing bounty, quietly enormous, for verified pieces of Ordane's work.

**The hedge**: the March's cunning-folk - cursebreakers, bone-setters, bee-whisperers. Unlicensed, indispensable, and being squeezed by the same edict. Mirre Kaltag is the best of them and the least careful.

**The roads**: veterans' bands half-turned to banditry, refugee columns still walking home, peddler dynasties, and the Wake's grave-circuits. News moves at walking pace and warps as it walks (the engine's rumor physics are this world's physics).

## Folk calendar and thin evenings (COMMON)

Quarter-days turn the year: **Seedmark** (spring), **Long Light** (midsummer), **the Reaping** (autumn), **Candlenight** (midwinter - debts come due, doors stay open for the dead, and nobody hunts). Dusk on a quarter-day is a THIN EVENING: the Neighbors walk, terms struck then bind double, and steel drawn then is remembered for a generation. The guild calendar prices work accordingly. Candlenight is ~40 days after the campaign opens: the Margravine's deadline, and the pack's first act has a clock in its bones.

## Sayings of the March (drop into any mouth)

- "Bury it right or feed it forever."
- "The war is over. Nobody told the war."
- "Salt eyes, dry heart" - unfair, common, and the Mark system in six words.
- "Never promise the fen anything you love."
- "A weir is patience with stones in it."
- "Terms kept are cheaper than stone walls" - and its tavern rejoinder: "A sword keeps terms with everything."

## Two of Tally's verses (LEARNED - she wrote them; use sparingly, one verse at a sitting)

*The Rushes* (banned in Gris, requested everywhere): "At the Field of Rushes the reeds grow tall, / they grow through helm and hand and all, / and when the wind combs down the shore / the rushes stand to ranks once more."

*The Weirman's Bride* (older than Tally, she added the last line): "She waits below the weir, my lads, she counts the stones we throw, / she kept the terms we broke for her in springs of long ago; / so greet her when you cross at dusk, and name her name with care - / for half the March forgot its debts, and half the debts are hers."

## What is actually true (HIDDEN - the cosmology under everything)

The Quiet Neighbors and the wrought are the same order of being at different ages: knots in the world's weave, tied by acts with weight - the old ones tied slowly by centuries of custom, the new ones torn fast by the war and the Muster's seam. TERMS work because a knot answers to what tied it; the READING works because Cup-brine carries a droplet of the same old working that the thin places leak - which is why the Toll pays in the same coin the wrought are made of: pieces of a person. Hunters are, by one droplet, the smallest of the Quiet Neighbors. Garrick knows this and has never taught it. Ordane worked it out from first principles, which is exactly what made the Hollow Muster possible - and exactly what he is assembling the pieces to finish properly this time (see secrets: the Author).
