# WEIRWATER - NPC Dossiers (GM)

Living Dossiers per GAME.md: these are Day-0 seeds - bonds, strain, and betrayal are purchased from receipts at the table, never scripted. Every named soul here runs on drives and can act off-screen; the rotation and world turns own their weeks.

## The Weirhouse

### Garrick Veld, last master of Blacklade
- **Body**: sixty-one; lame left leg (Cindermoor's fire, year 5); brine-eyes gone nearly white; hands still fast for three seconds and no more.
- **Drives**: keep the house alive past him; make Sile and the player better than he was; die at the weir, not in the fen.
- **Toll paid (nine lines)**: taste of bread, the color blue, his sister's face, birdsong above a certain pitch, the smell of rain, warmth in the left hand, the tune of the Weirman's Bride (he remembers every word, no melody), tears (he cannot), and one he will not name even to the player. He hears the Quiet on still nights and answers it politely, which frightens him.
- **Knows (HIDDEN)**: the true cosmology (hunters are a droplet of the same old working - he worked it out decades ago and never taught it, judging the knowledge to be Ordane-shaped); the library sales to Sjunna; that the last two Cup deaths were WRONG and he has been too afraid of the answer to investigate.
- **Voice**: economical; teaches by question; swears only in guild ledger-terms ("that man is a bad entry").
- **Fracture**: his shame about the library vs his doctrine of the recorded truth. The player finding the empty shelves before he confesses is a strain-event; his confession, if earned, is one of the pack's quiet summits.

### Sile of the Fen, novice
- **Body**: nineteen; Cup last spring (survived alone of three - the other two are the poisoned-Cup thread, and she does not know it); eyes still turning, one more pale than the other; fen-born, swims like a lie.
- **Drives**: prove the Cup chose right; learn everything faster than it can be taught; find out what happened to the two who drank with her.
- **Toll paid**: one line - the smell of her mother's kitchen. She writes it on her arm some mornings so the loss stays chosen.
- **Arc**: the pack's apprentice-mirror. Her first solo contract (a fenwight; starting board) goes exactly as well as what the player has modeled. If the player runs butcher-grain, Sile gets efficient; mender-grain, she gets brave. Arc 2's endings decide her title; the player decides her doctrine.
- **Voice**: blunt, fen-cadence, allergic to ceremony; the only person who asks Garrick direct questions.

### Pip, stable-orphan
- **Body**: eleven-ish (he sets his own age by usefulness); quick, small, permanently underfoot; feeds Burr contraband apples.
- **Drives**: belong somewhere; the Cup someday (three-in-five dead and he knows the number better than anyone - both parents are in the war's unmarked math); matter to the player.
- **Function**: the morality meter. What the player teaches Pip - by lesson or by being watched - is the campaign's conscience track, and Garrick, Sile, Halm, and the March are all reading it. His Cup question comes due in the endgame of Arc 2, whatever the answer costs.

### Burr, horse
- **Body**: war-teamster stock, seventeen hands of skeptical; ten winters old plus the six since; scars catalogued in his file like any veteran.
- **Function**: kit home (worn/carried/stashed lives on his tack by the kit grammar); war-sense as instrument (his refusals are information the fiction may consult - Burr does not read natures, he reads AMBUSH); expense line (feed is registered and burns). He is written into the record like a companion because he is one. If Burr dies, it is an EVENT, priced like one, and the March hears of it.

## The road companions

### Tally Voss, balladeer and fixer
- **Body**: thirties, road-weathered, lute older than the war and better maintained than she is.
- **Drives**: stay ahead of four debts (Drossen card-room, Gris printer of unprintable songs, a peddler dynasty, and one she lies about); collect every story the war left; matter more than a singer is allowed to.
- **Ledger**: her ballad-memory is a lore-engine - names, dates, who marched where, which village buried whom. Treat her as a walking Wakehouse register with a tempo. She skims contract finder's fees (ten percent, calls it eight).
- **Knot (HIDDEN)**: the desertion. Year 9, she walked from the Ostravend levy the night before her company was fed to the Rushes campaign - every man she sang for that night is in the field she will not go near. Her fourth debt is paid yearly to a widow in Sallowmere. TERMS exist (the pack's rule applied to a person): the Rushes visited, the names sung once, aloud, there. The Great Wake ending hands her this scene whether she is ready or not.
- **Voice**: performs constantly except when it matters; the drop into her real register is the tell the player learns to watch for.

### Mirre Kaltag, cursebreaker
- **Body**: forties, hedge-plain by choice (the Collegium robes are folded at the bottom of a chest she keeps anyway); hands scarred by wire from the southern anchor.
- **Drives**: find Ordane; unmake the instruments; never again hold a line for someone else's working; keep the player at exactly arm's length (the arm shortens by receipts only).
- **History (HIDDEN, surfacing through Arc 3)**: Ordane's student twenty years ago - the best he had, which she knows and hates knowing; war-licensed as a counter-working anchor; sole survivor of the southern anchor at the Rushes; license burned by her own hand at the peace, in front of a Collegium panel, with commentary. Weiss has the transcript and rereads it.
- **Craft**: the March's best living reader of WORKINGS (not natures - the two crafts overlap like neighboring farms and argue like them too). She can do things the player cannot; the reverse is also true and it costs her something to say so.
- **Romance**: possible, adult, on her exact terms, renegotiated honestly or ended cleanly. She does not soften with intimacy; she becomes more precise. Betrayal of her trust is purchasable from receipts and unrecoverable.
- **Voice**: measured, technical, devastating in short sentences. Says "my teacher" and never the name until the day she says the name.

### Brother Halm, Wakepriest (defrocked)
- **Body**: enormous, slow, hands like grave-spades because they are; sleeps outdoors by preference "so the door stays open."
- **Drives**: bury everything right; earn nothing (he holds his defrocking to be correct in law and irrelevant in doctrine); protect Pip's laugh, specifically, as a project.
- **Function**: rites are LOAD-BEARING in this world - Halm makes half the bestiary's terms performable at need, and the Great Wake ending is impossible without him or the peace he has to broker with the order that expelled him (his hidden knot: he wants readmission and believes wanting it is vanity).
- **Voice**: liturgy-cadenced plain speech; the pack's gentleness, so that when Halm goes quiet the table knows the weather changed.

## The powers

### Ulla Carel, Margravine of Gris
- **Drives**: a profitable, quiet province; no second war on her watch; no unlicensed workings embarrassing her in dispatches to the capital.
- **Truth**: not a villain - a government. She will enforce the debt because the ledger says so, extend it if given lawful reason, and crush what threatens order without appetite either way. She finds the Weirwatch archaic, useful, and cheap at the price - her private position, discoverable, is that licensing Blacklade is tidier than killing it. She did NOT order the Cup poisoning, and her learning of it is a live grenade the player can choose when to hand her.
- **Voice**: reads while you talk; answers the paragraph, not the sentence.

### Lector Abram Weiss, Collegium magus in residence
- **Drives**: the Proscribed List (Ordane's name at its top - his commission and his obsession: his cohort broke at the Rushes holding the counter-working); the instruments recovered UNSTUDIED into Collegium vaults; his own dread, managed daily, that hedge-craft and guild-craft work as well as they do without theory.
- **Texture**: rigorous, honest by his own lights, contemptuous of the hedge, secretly keeps a file of cases where the guild's terms outperformed Collegium method - labeled ANOMALIES. Can become an ally of iron usefulness or a lawful antagonist of pure friction, by receipts. Holds the Whitstan file, including the old Cup-poisoning method, and has not connected it to Blacklade's dead candidates yet. When he does, his choice of what to do with it is one of the pack's hinges - and it will be computed from how the player has treated him.

### Odo Brant, clerk-of-licenses (Arc 2's small villain)
- **Drives**: the under-magistracy at Drossen; being taken seriously by people who read; never being touched by consequences.
- **Method**: found the Whitstan method in the files Weiss let him index. Sources grave-brine adulterant through a Gris apothecary. Keeps the receipts (he is a clerk; it does not occur to him not to). A small man doing a monstrous thing tidily - the pack's argument that the March's worst knots are tied by people, and its cleanest terms-instead-of-steel case against a human being.

### Velm Ordane, the Author
- **Presence**: rumor, then handwriting, then instruments, then a chair across a table - pace him like a bestiary entry and let the study system work on him; he is the campaign's final Reading, and the Toll for reading HIM deep is the worst price on the sheet.
- **Drives**: correction (the Muster done right: every dead thing settled, permanently, everywhere - the Wake's paradise imposed by working); the instruments; the Wake-Child, once he learns it exists; being UNDERSTOOD (his one human hunger, and the door every negotiation ending walks through).
- **Texture**: unfailingly courteous; asks good questions and listens to answers; grieves the Muster's dead as HIS dead, which is both true and the exact shape of what is wrong with him. He does not tempt with power; he tempts with REST - yours, the March's, everyone's. Write him so the Author's-Terms ending is genuinely arguable at the table.
- **Voice**: a teacher's patience; the only character who talks about the war entirely in the future tense.
