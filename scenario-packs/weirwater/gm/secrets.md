# WEIRWATER - GM Secrets

SPOILERS. Player-facing text must never contain unflipped facts from this file. Each secret lists its discovery vectors: secrets in this pack are CASES - they surrender to the same study system as the wrought.

## The Wake-Child (Arc 1's engine)

**The Muster's residue pooled in a living child**, born on the Field of Rushes in the war's last winter, mother dead of the birth. The child - called LULLE by whoever currently keeps her, a grandmother-figure named Wenna who is not kin and knows something is wrong - is six, walking west in the refugee columns, and does not know what she is. Wrought quicken in her wake; the unnamed (bestiary) are the Muster trying to re-muster through her. She is not dangerous the way anyone expects: she is a seam, not a spell. She likes birds, which go quiet near her, and she has learned not to mention it.

- **Vectors**: the case-pattern on the map (hollow soldiers and unnamed thickening along the east road); the failed fenwight terms (first unnamed case); road-rumor of the lucky child; the moss-hag (knows exactly what walked past her bounds and will sell it dearly); a deep Reading of any unnamed (two Toll payments, shows the BORROWED cause and the direction it was borrowed from).
- **Timing law**: the child stays offstage, and the first full unnamed case stays unplayed, until the player has learned to trust the study system (three-plus clean cases minimum). The Day-0 east-road prints are a tease that dead-ends honestly (see starting-state) - a dot for the map, never the reveal. The rules must work before they break.

## The poisoned Cup (Arc 2's crime)

**Odo Brant is killing Cup candidates.** Method from the Whitstan file (indexed for Weiss, read at leisure): an adulterant in the grave-brine - the deaths read as ordeal-failure unless someone who knows the true weeping death examines them. Rin and Cobb, Sile's Cup-mates, died convulsive and fast: wrong. Brant's chain: a Gris apothecary (Master Foss, believes he is selling rat-bane to a granary), a bribed weir-lad who salts the draw-jar (Dannic, seventeen, half-aware and terrified), and receipts for all of it in Brant's own tidy hand, filed under a false granary account.

- **Vectors**: exhumation with Halm (rites-lawful and he will insist on them) shows the wrong death; Sile's testimony (she tasted iron; the weeping Cup tastes of nothing - guild lore, Garrick can confirm and go white doing it); the draw-jar's residue read by Mirre or Sjunna; Dannic cracks under any kindness; the paper trail is a clerk's - Tally's Gris contacts or the Margravine's own audit find it in a week once pointed.
- **The terms option**: Brant is the pack's human terms-case: exposure to Carel (lawful ruin), to Weiss (whose file it was - his choice then becomes a hinge), or the player's own justice (butcher-grain, and the March hears). Killing him quietly makes the guild safer and the player smaller; the pack takes no side aloud.

## The library (Garrick's shame)

**Blacklade's library is at Sjunna's, sold volume by volume against the debt's interest.** Sjunna has sold nothing on: every book is marked "held" in her ledger, at purchase price, no interest - a mercy she will deny to the player's face because mercy is bad for her market. Garrick's confession, if the player earns it rather than forces it, is Arc 2's quiet summit; the books coming home, however that is engineered, is the Weir-Holds ending's best scene.

- **Vectors**: the gaps read wrong on the shelves (a hunter's eye); Sjunna's ledger (she shows exactly one page if asked a fair question fairly); Pip knows and has been carrying it alone, which is its own small rescue.

## The Author (Arc 3's engine)

**Ordane lives**, as "Magister Velte," an itinerant scrivener-scholar of no fixed address, six years into retrieving the Muster's six instruments. Held now: the surveyor's chain, one lesser (a mustering-whistle). Seeking: the drum (sold at the Drossen auction to a tollhouse keeper whose door now has a perfect hollow-soldier sentry), the roll-that-reads-its-names (in the Gris Wakehouse archive, miscatalogued as a relic of the Rushes - the Prioress half-suspects), and two lesser (a lantern; a pay-chest lid) moving through Sjunna's market grapevine. His correction thesis, his courtesy, and his hunger to be understood: see npcs. He learns of the Wake-Child in the campaign's middle act unless the player's own noise teaches him earlier.

- **Vectors**: Mirre's questions (she is one instrument ahead of the player and two behind Ordane); instrument-warped local cases; the moss-hag (his oldest acquaintance in the March; her price for his description is a promise, exact); Weiss's bounty-book postings mirror-image the same trail.
- **The Rushes truth (deepest layer)**: what the Collegium's counter-working cost - it was anchored in living volunteers and conscripts, and the southern anchor's circuit was fed to it knowingly. Mirre survived it; Weiss's cohort staffed it; the Collegium's silence about the price is why Ordane believes no one has standing to judge his arithmetic. Every faction's file on the Rushes is missing the same page, and the Great Wake ending reads that page aloud.

## Smaller knots (one line each, expandable at the table)

- **Tally's fourth debt**: the Sallowmere widow - see npcs; terms exist and the Rushes is their address. Vectors: the ballad she never finishes past the second verse (any request for The Rushes' whole song); a Sallowmere local greeting her by a levy-company nickname; Halm noticing she reroutes around the Rushes road every single time; the yearly payment itself, if the player ever handles her purse.
- **Halm's vanity**: he wants readmission to the order he defies; the Prioress would say yes to the right penance, and the penance is the pilgrimage he will hate.
- **Burr's brand**: under the mud, an Ostravend levy-brand - Kest's horse was the enemy's once, which a sharp-eyed veteran (Vey) will notice aloud at a delicate moment.
- **The weir itself**: the oldest treaty-stone in the March is its keystone; the moss-hag holds its terms, the guild has forgotten them, and the unmaking of instruments at the weir (Rolls-Closed ending) calls that treaty due.
